Observation / DEX competition

Market expansion and share gains answer different questions

Reported DEX volume changed -13.5% across consecutive 30-day windows. Parent-group HHI is 1,616, a +156-point change from the start of the latest 30-day interval. A larger venue can grow while losing share in an expanding market; concentration and absolute growth therefore need separate checks.

Within the stable cohort, Metric has the largest share-reallocation effect ($6.04B). This is volume relative to a constant-share counterfactual, not evidence of customer acquisition or a causal product advantage.

The stable cohort covers 84.0% of prior and 81.9% of current volume. Changing coverage can alter the apparent market trajectory. Inspect the reconciled accounting decomposition →

Observation / CEX activity

Decompose notional growth into activity and ticket size

Across the four selected USDT pairs, 30-day quote volume changed -18.1% and reported trade count changed -3.2%. Aggregate quote value per reported trade moved from 364.90 to 308.48 USDT.

The exact identity allocates the volume change to -2.29B USDT from trade count at the old average ticket, and -10.86B USDT from the change in average ticket at the new count. This ordering assigns the interaction to ticket size.

A ticket-size change can reflect price changes, symbol mix or execution splitting; it does not identify larger investors or organic demand. Counts are executions, not traders. Check the identity and its limits →

Observation / Risk

Activity is not execution capacity

The latest 30-day annualized daily-return volatility ranges from 36.8% for BTCUSDT to 50.1% for ETHUSDT. This estimator measures close-to-close price variation and misses intraday paths.

High traded volume and a tight displayed spread do not establish low price impact for a large order. The market-quality panel tests finite order sizes against the observed book and marks incomplete depth bands. Its snapshots were not observed throughout the candle history.

Hypotheses / Not tested causally

What evidence would strengthen these findings?

Liquidity resilience: the hypothesis that volatile periods have thinner books needs repeated, time-aligned book observations, a fixed sampling schedule and controls for time of day. A daily REST snapshot cannot support that conclusion.

Flow and returns: test whether lagged aggressor imbalance predicts subsequent returns using chronological train/test splits, transaction costs and multiple-testing controls. Same-period buy ratios and returns do not establish direction of causation.

DEX income quality: test comparable protocol-owned income only after auditing fee recipients, incentives and adapter regime changes. Exact ID matching alone cannot create an economically consistent cohort.

V3 · Cross-market volatility regimes

Research Question

Do DEX and four-pair CEX activity co-move around high-volatility days?

Hypothesis

Common market shocks can coincide with activity changes in both universes.

Method

Synchronise complete UTC days. High regime means equal-weight four-pair minute RV above the 80th percentile of earlier days only (minimum 30). Same-day classification is retrospective. Select complete [-2,+2] event windows at least five days apart.

Robustness

Contemporaneous and forward slopes are tested separately above, with HAC lag sensitivities and a chronological benchmark. Event paths remain descriptive.

Limitations

Common shocks and endogenous event selection prevent causal interpretation. Five-day separated event windows; descriptive paths only; too few events for reliable inference. DEX USD and selected CEX USDT pairs have incompatible coverage. No market share, migration, causal transmission or historical order-book condition is inferred.

Business Implications

Common activity bursts support joint operational risk monitoring; venue-specific capacity or acquisition decisions require additional liquidity history, user data and attributable exposures.
