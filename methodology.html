Definitions before conclusions.

Source contracts, financial formulas, sampling boundaries and validation rules for a reproducible crypto market research portfolio.

DEX historical window: 2025-11-14 — 2026-11-13 UTC. CEX historical window: 2025-11-14 — 2026-11-13 UTC. UTC is the exchange candle boundary, not the reader's local date. Book and trade timestamps are shown separately.

Research contract

Observations are calculations on the stated source and reporting universe. Hypotheses propose a mechanism or a future test. Causal conclusions require an identification design beyond this descriptive portfolio; none are claimed here. Accounting identities reconcile quantities but do not identify causes.

The DefiLlama DEX universe and four Binance Spot USDT pairs are not interchangeable denominators. The site does not estimate Binance-wide volume, CEX-versus-DEX market share, organic activity or unique participation. USDT values are not silently labeled as USD.

Sources & granularity

  • DefiLlama public overview APIs: dailyVolume for DEXs, dailyFees and dailyRevenue. Current volume metadata defines the Dexs universe; endpoint-specific names resolve to exact DefiLlama IDs. Official definitions · Detailed existing DEX audit.
  • Binance Spot public market-data-only API: data-api.binance.vision/api/v3/klines, exchangeInfo, time, depth and aggTrades. No trading API key or signed request. Official market-data reference · Market-data-only service.
  • Historical completeness: daily candles are checked for every expected UTC day per symbol, including 30 warm-up days. This establishes response calendar completeness, not independent verification of exchange executions.
  • Microstructure: one retrieved book per pair per refresh and a bounded five-minute trade request. These are discrete observations, not a continuous reconstructed order book. Source responses may be revised or delayed.

Binance daily metric definitions

Quote volume is Binance Kline field 7, measured in USDT for these pairs. Trade count is field 8; it counts reported trades, not traders or API aggregate rows. Taker-buy quote volume is field 10. The quote-weighted buy ratio is Σ taker-buy quote / Σ quote volume; a zero denominator yields N/A. It is never the unweighted average of daily ratios.

Rolling trends use trailing 7 or 30 complete calendar days, including the labeled day. Volume and trade-count curves are daily means; ratio curves divide rolling sums. Thirty-day volume growth compares current and previous non-overlapping 30-day sums. Price return is close(t) / close(t−30) − 1.

Realized volatility uses daily close-to-close log returns and a trailing 30-return root-mean-square estimator, annualized with 365:

r(t) = ln(close(t) / close(t−1))
RV30(t) = √[365 × (Σ r² over 30 complete returns) / 30]

The return mean is not subtracted. This is a daily-grid estimator of realized variance, not a forecast, implied volatility or an intraday-integrated measure. Thirty prior candles supply the first displayed day's return window. Future and incomplete UTC days are rejected.

Activity and average-ticket decomposition

For each consecutive 30-day window, sum quote volume Q and trade counts N across the same four pairs. Set A = Q/N. The displayed identity is:

Q₁ − Q₀ = A₀ × (N₁ − N₀) + N₁ × (A₁ − A₀)

The first term holds the prior average ticket fixed; the second uses the current count and therefore includes the interaction. Alternative ordering changes the allocation, not the reconciled total. Symbol mix, asset price and execution splitting affect average ticket; no user-size or causal claim follows.

Market-microstructure definitions & sampling

Spread: 10,000 × (best ask − best bid) / mid, where mid = (best ask + best bid)/2. The API supplies a last-update ID; REST retrieval start/end times bracket client observation. Neither time is an exchange-issued book timestamp. Books for different pairs are sequential requests, not synchronized snapshots.

Depth: sum price × base quantity at bids within 10, 50 or 100 basis points below mid and asks within those bands above mid. Each side is complete only if the furthest returned level reaches the requested boundary. If a returned side does not reach it, its displayed total is a lower bound. The API limit is 1,000 levels per side; absence of farther levels is not proof that the entire book ends there.

Imbalance: (bid depth − ask depth)/(bid depth + ask depth) for a common band, only when both sides are complete and combined depth is positive. Zero is balanced displayed notional; it does not predict price direction.

Simulated impact: for 10,000, 100,000 and 1,000,000 USDT targets, a buy consumes asks until its quote budget is spent; a sell consumes bids for a base quantity equal to target quote divided by the pre-trade mid. Compute buy impact 10,000 × (VWAP/mid − 1) and sell impact 10,000 × (1 − VWAP/mid). Partial fills show their fill fraction and N/A impact; they are never treated as full execution estimates. Spread crossing is included. Fees, latency, queue position, hidden liquidity, cancellations and replenishment are excluded.

Aggressor-side flow: m=true marks a buyer who is maker, hence seller-initiated execution. Quote flow is price × quantity; net flow is buy minus sell. Underlying trade count sums last trade ID − first trade ID + 1 for retained aggregate rows; row count is reported separately.

Bounded sampling: request a recent five-minute window using Binance server time, with at most three pages of 1,000 aggregate records per pair. Forward pagination and timestamp filtering retain only the requested interval. An explicit page-cap flag and completeness proof accompany each sample. A completed requested window is not a complete day. A cap can concentrate retained observations near the start of the requested window; no extrapolation or daily buy-flow inference is made. First/last trade span is not a percentage of time covered.

Retries and limits: request attempts and pages are bounded. Retryable network/server failures and HTTP 429 use backoff and Retry-After; bans and access restrictions fail the build. No endpoint rotation or access-restriction bypass is attempted. Any failure of required coverage prevents publication and leaves the last successfully published website available.

Preserved DEX definitions & economic limits

The DEX models retain reported USD volume, protocol/parent shares, fees, revenue, matched reporting coverage, HHI, CR5, stable-cohort growth allocation and trailing robust anomalies. HHI is 10,000 × Σ parent share². Shares include every observed parent group, not just the plotted leaders. Missing protocol observations remain missing; explicit zeros remain zero. No parent totals are added to child totals.

Growth allocation holds a 60-day consistently reporting cohort: ΔVᵢ = sᵢ₀ × ΔM + M₁ × Δsᵢ. Cohort coverage is shown for both windows. Anomalies compare log(1+volume) to the prior 30-day median, require 20 observations and use scale max(1.4826 × MAD, 0.10); flags need |z| ≥ 4 and |deviation| ≥ 50%. Neither technique proves manipulation or causation.

ID matching is not economic harmonization. Fees and revenue are adapter-reported concepts, and reporting cohorts differ. HumidiFi economics are excluded because its adapter uses an inventory-P&L proxy. Signed revenue adjustments remain signed. Uniswap V4 revenue may be booked under V3; a version-level zero does not establish no economic income. Aerodrome Slipstream definitions include incentive/bribe flows and changed over time. Cross-version ownership and regime changes prevent interpreting all matched basis points as a uniform trading fee or profitability measure. Review the Uniswap V4 and Aerodrome Slipstream adapters before selecting a comparable economic cohort.

Current Dexs taxonomy is not a historical constituent census; survivorship, revisions, missing reporting and adapter changes affect comparisons. See all existing validation gates and metric definitions.

Validation & reproducibility

Publication requires symbol status and quote-asset checks; exact daily candle count and millisecond UTC boundaries; finite/nonnegative volumes; consistent high/low/open/close values; bounded taker volumes and integral counts; valid, sorted, uncrossed books; and valid, deduplicated trade IDs/timestamps. Missing days are not interpolated. Historical gaps, malformed source schemas and exhausted required API requests fail closed.

Private response snapshots retain hashes, request parameters and retrieval timestamps. DuckDB models keep source grains separate. A complete validated website is built in staging before replacing output, and the publication script permits only reviewed static page assets. Source code, raw responses, credentials, CSVs and databases are excluded from the public repository. There is no browser-side API request.

Repeated refreshes are discrete samples, not a retrospective liquidity backfill. Durable historical order-book research would require deliberate retention and a documented sampling design; this release does not claim a continuous order-book history.
